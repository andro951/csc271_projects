Banana Brown Sugar Cookies

This is a weird recipe I came up with a few years ago. Initially, we were just out of eggs, so I tried to find a substitute for eggs, and applesauce worked pretty well. Although, the cookies tasted a bit like apples which wasn't great. Later, I decided to try making cookie dough that was safe to eat because I prefer cookie dough over cookies. I like bananas and they are very cheap, so I decided to use bananas as a substitute for eggs. I also like brown sugar, so I decided to make them banana brown sugar cookie dough. After trying it, still getting a terrible stomach ache after, and looking it up online, I found out you would have to bake the flour to make it safe to eat raw, so that idea was kind of a bust. But, I still like the cookies, so I kept the recipe.

Ingredient Quantity
3 Large Banana
3 cups Brown Sugar
1/4 Sugar
2 sticks Butter
1 tbsp Vanilla Extract
5 cups Flower
1 tsp Baking Soda
1/2 tsp Baking Powder
1 tsp Salt

Arroz e Feijão com Frango or Brazilian Rice and Beans with Chicken

My favorite dish is a simple Brazilian classic: Arroz e Feijão com Frango or Rice and Beans with Chicken

Other than it tasting good, it reminds me of my childhood. Just the smell brings me back to simpler times when I would for my mom to prepare this dish.

It's also the most commonly eaten dish in Brazil so I know I'm not the only one that thinks this dish is special.

You can eat it plainly or use it as a base to add more food!

Ingredients Amount
Rice 150g
Beans 150g
Garlic 6 cloves
Onion 2 medium
Chicken 150g
Salt 4 tbsp
Brazilian Spice 2 tbsp

Jollof Rice

I am of African descent, more specifically I am Nigerian, and one dish that had become a big part of my pallate growing up had to be Jollof Rice. Every other week, either on a Wednesday or a Friday, I would come home from school finding my mother cooking Jollof Rice, and some days, that Jollof Rice would get me out of the worst of moods and the worst of days. Usually, the Jollof Recipe is served with some form of meat, usually beef or chicken, but I've tried other variations of Jollof Rice that were made with Guinea fowl or even shrimp.
